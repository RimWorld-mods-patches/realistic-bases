# Offline defense simulation: findings for the defense planner

2026-09-30 · Evidence from the milestone-1 harness described in
[`plans/2026-09-29-defense-sim-threat-field.md`](plans/2026-09-29-defense-sim-threat-field.md).
Code: branch `defense-sim-harness` (`Source/RealisticBases.DefenseSim*`).

## What this evidence is, and is not

- **Synthetic, offline, approximate.** Seeded synthetic maps planned by the real
  pure planner (deferred defense pass); hand-entered combat numbers
  (`defense-sim-combat.v1`, source-reviewed approximations of 1.6 Core, not
  game-equivalent); a deterministic expected-damage rollout, not a tick engine.
  Nothing here was observed in RimWorld.
- **Adversarial.** The attacker is the player's 5-pawn squad (2 sniper rifles,
  3 assault rifles, flak) choosing, per spawn point, the best of ~18 plans
  (route weight × sniper overwatch × rush/fight) against the known defense.
  That is a stress bound, stronger than an average player.
- **Objective (from the design discussion).** The player's pawns are precious:
  a breach bought with ≥3 downed attackers counts as a defensive success.
  Headline metric: **cheap breaches** = breaches with <3 attackers down, on
  32 held-out spawn points per map that the optimizer never saw.
- **Relaxed placement.** Defenses are optimized without production route/
  service rules (gate access is kept). Winners are evidence for principles,
  not spawnable plans.
- **Defender controller.** Residents have a staging post per front, react 5 s
  after the raid starts, walk out through a gate, and once the squad commits to
  a breach point walk to the nearest cell that covers it (`Respond`).
- Reference budget ("×1"): 8 mini-turrets + 1 autocannon allowance + 18
  sandbags + ammo + power ≈ 5.4k silver. Caps: 40 walls, 40 barricades,
  60 sandbags. 7 residents unless stated (6 ranged; with a mortar court the
  Support resident crews the mortar instead).

Runs: 800 annealing iterations per scenario unless stated; plains seeds 7–9,
obstacles and clutter seeds 1–3; Mid = `IndustrialMid`, Late = `IndustrialLate`.
Raw outputs: `.runtime/defense-sim/` (ignored), regenerated by the runner.

## Headline findings

Numbers are held-out cheap breaches (lower is better for the settlement) unless
stated. Group comparisons come from separate optimizations and carry the
search noise measured in finding 9 (±15 points on one map); ablations are
paired (same layout, one element removed) and are the stronger evidence.

### 1. The residents are the defense; turrets multiply them

Paired ablations of the optimized layouts:

| Removed | ×1 budget (608 attacks) | ×4 budget (192 attacks) |
|---|---|---|
| nothing (champion) | 489 (80%) | 10 (5%) |
| added walls | 534 | 21 |
| sandbags + barricades | 540 | 12 |
| all structures | 554 | 21 |
| all turrets | 605 (99.5%) | 192 (100%) |
| all residents | 608 (100%) | 184 (96%) |
| residents hold posts (no response) | 608 (100%) | 180 (94%) |

Neither turrets nor residents alone stop a raid; together they do. Resident
count is the strongest single lever at ×1 (plains, Mid): 4 residents → 99%
cheap breaches, 7 → 71%, 12 → 11%. **Planner implication:** turret placement
must be designed around where residents will fight, and the defense budget of a
small settlement buys little without people.

### 2. Residents must move to the threat

Static posts: 100% cheap breaches (plains Mid ×1), against 71% for the
responding controller with the same budget. Knob sweep on frozen layouts
(288 combinations, training spawns): forbidding interception costs ~20 extra
cheap breaches per 32 attacks; capping the walk at 20/40 cells costs ~14/9.
Retreating wounded residents (30%/60% HP) is harmful (+6/+8 per 32): a
withdrawn rifleman is a lost gun. The focus-fire rule is irrelevant.
Committing ~25 route cells before the breach point is the best average; the
best value varies by map. Sending 1–2 counter-snipers helps on some maps and
hurts on others. Tuned tactics alone saved 5–9 cheap breaches per 96 attacks;
re-optimizing the layout under tuned tactics saved another 10–26 (within search
noise). **Planner/P3 implication:** P3's bounded local response should allow
long intercept walks to the committed breach point; do not add wounded
withdrawal as a default.

### 3. Budget: ×1 is far too small for this attacker; ×4 is enough

| Budget | Cheap breaches | Any breach | Attackers down | Defenders down | Spent |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ×1 (≈5.4k) | 80% | 94% | 1.1 | 6.7 | 5.3k |
| ×2 | 23% | 69% | 2.4 | 4.6 | 10.8k |
| ×4 | 4% | 41% | 3.0 | 3.0 | 21.2k |
| ×8 | 1% | 26% | 2.4 | 5.1 | 33.2k (of 43k) |

(plains s7–8, Mid+Late, 128 attacks each.) The optimizer spends nearly the
whole budget up to ×4 and leaves ~23% unspent at ×8, where the structure caps
bind. Beyond ×4 the attacker stops breaching and switches to safe sniper
attrition, so attacker losses fall again and defender losses rise.
**Planner implication:** for a 7-resident industrial settlement to punish a
5-pawn player raid, the defense allowance should be roughly 3–4× today's
reference package, or the roster larger.

### 4. Turrets per attacked front: about 8 is the threshold

Across all Respond runs, held-out attacks by the number of turrets on the
attacked front: 0 → 72% cheap breaches, 1–3 → 63–91%, 4–7 → 38–63%,
**8+ → 6%** (1,948 attacks; confounded with budget). At ×1 the optimizer
concentrates 4–7 turrets on one or two fronts and effectively concedes the
others (fronts with 0–1 turrets were breached in 90–100% of attacks).
**Planner implication:** spreading two mini-turrets per side is the wrong
shape; either a front has a concentrated battery of ~8 mutually supporting
guns or it relies on responders.

### 5. Where the optimizer puts things (motifs; hypotheses)

- **Turrets stand off the wall, not on it:** median 9 cells out (IQR 5–13),
  only 4–7% touching the settlement wall; median 17–19 cells from the nearest
  gate exit; 92–100% within range of another turret (mutual support); ~40%
  with cover beside them; nearest resident post median 7 cells.
- **Turret types:** at ×1 almost only mini-turrets (159 of 168) even where
  Late could buy autocannons; at ×4 Late buys some sniper turrets and
  autocannons, and the Late ×4 champions nearly eliminate *any* breach
  (8/96 vs 77/96 for Mid ×4), i.e. long-range guns turn costly breaches into
  repulses.
- **Structures:** ~50 sandbags, ~35 barricades, ~25 walls, mostly 8–10 cells
  out and rarely at gates (2–6%). Only 30–38% of cover is beside a shooter, so
  much of it is likely neutral filler accepted by the annealer (its cost weight
  is tiny); the ablations above bound its real value at ~5–10 points.
- **Staging posts:** ~8 cells out, 65% with adjacent cover, and only ~50% on
  their own front: with responders, a central staging point near the gates
  (short walk anywhere) beats a forward post on "its" side.
- **Attacker behaviour:** at ×1 the best response is mostly the whole squad
  pushing and fighting (WithSquad 74%); at ×4 overwatch sniper plans rise to
  46%, i.e. snipers out-ranging the defense become the attacker's main tool as
  defenses strengthen.

### 6. The mortar matters as counter-sniper insurance, limited by accuracy

Settlement mortar (court + Support crew, 12 HE shells, forced-miss radius 9,
indirect fire at attackers stationary ≥5 s beyond 29.9 cells). Paired
ablation over all mortar runs: removing it raises cheap breaches 285 → 318 of
448 and cuts damage dealt to attackers 142 → 99. Shells fired per attack are
~5–9, so stock is not the limit (40 shells ≈ 12 shells). Accuracy is: a miss
radius of 5 instead of 9 halves cheap breaches (81% → 38%, plains Mid ×1). At
×4, adding the mortar turns most remaining breaches into repulses (any breach
77/96 → 16/96). The forced-miss model and 15 s cycle are the least certain
numbers here and should be checked against 1.6 source before relying on them.
**Planner implication:** keep the mortar court; its value is punishing
stationary snipers and bashing squads; more shells are not worth budget.

### 7. Map edge distance is not the explanation it looked like

On a single map the fastest breaches came from a side ~15 cells from the map
edge, but across 608 held-out attacks the cheap-breach rate does not fall with
route length (20–39 cells: 65%, 60–79: 91%, 140+: 59%). Turret concentration
per front (finding 4) explains far more. Edge setback is not a supported
planner lever from this evidence.

### 8. The shipped defense and map recipes

The as-shipped Production defense (4 mini-turrets, barricades, static posts,
own host) was cheaply breached in 2,096 of 2,144 held-out attacks (98%)
across 67 scenarios.
Obstacles and clutter maps behave like plains (77% / 82% at ×1, Mid+Late).
The `mountain` recipe was rejected by the planner on all seeds tried
(`no_valid_layout`) and is not yet a usable cohort.

### 9. Limits of the evidence

- **Search noise is large.** Same map, four search seeds: 72%, 81%, 88%, 97%
  cheap breaches; 3,000 iterations did not beat 800. Per-map optima are not
  well converged; differences under ~15 points between single runs are noise.
- **Overfitting:** training cheap-breach rates are consistently better than
  held-out; only held-out numbers are reported.
- **Omniscient attacker** (knows the defense and chooses the best of ~18 plans
  per spawn); no attacker retreat.
- **Combat numbers are approximations**; no suppression, healing, fire, weather,
  destructible cover or reopened sight lines in the evaluator.

### 10. Stochastic tick check of the deterministic evaluator

Champions re-scored by the tick verifier (0.1 s steps, discrete bursts,
per-shot hit and armor rolls, sampled mortar landings; the attacker re-picks
its plan per spawn from 32 paired seeds), held-out spawns:

| Group | Mean-field cheap breaches | Tick expected cheap breaches | Tick any breach | Spawns disagreeing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| plains Mid ×1 | 81% | 82% | 86% | 11/96 |
| plains Late ×1 | 71% | 70% | 83% | 10/96 |
| obstacles Mid ×1 | 90% | 82% | 88% | 6/96 |
| plains Mid ×4 | 3% | **12%** | 22% | 5/96 |

The deterministic evaluator ranks weak and average defenses correctly, but is
**optimistic for strong ones**: dice let some attacks through that expected
damage stops (one ×4 map: 0% → 24%). It also under-counts attacker losses
(ticks: 1.3–2.8 down vs 0.1–1.3). **Implication:** verify finalists with ticks,
and add margin to any budget or turret-count threshold derived from
mean-field numbers (finding 3's ×4 is closer to 12% cheap breaches than 4%).

## Recommendations for the defense planner (to validate in game)

1. Size defense to people: plan resident count and responder behaviour first;
   turrets multiply residents, they do not replace them.
2. Concentrate turrets into mutually supporting batteries of ~8 on the likely
   attack fronts, set ~5–13 cells out from the wall with cover, instead of
   two per side.
3. Give responders unlimited intercept walks to the committed breach point;
   stage them centrally near gates; no automatic wounded withdrawal.
4. Budget ~3–4× today's reference package for a 7-resident industrial
   settlement (tick check: ~12% cheap breaches remain at ×4), or grow the roster; Late settlements should buy long-range
   guns (sniper turret/autocannon) once the budget allows.
5. Keep the mortar court; do not spend on extra shells.
6. Treat structure counts as secondary; cover beside shooters is the part
   with measurable value.

## Next steps

- Ablations that move one element (turret-to-wall distance, battery
  concentration vs spread) rather than delete it, at matched budget.
- Better-converged search (restarts, larger archive) to cut the ±15-point noise.
- Tick-sim v1: per-pawn positions and spread, destructible cover, turret
  explosions, suppression; attacker retreat.
- Only if tuned rule-based tactics plateau: an offline learned policy as a
  research tool, distilled back into readable rules. A small learned
  surrogate that pre-screens layouts is the cheaper use of a network.

# P4D — Quest-location support

2026-10-01 · Implementation active · P4D.0 native baseline recorded

## Outcome and initial support set

Use the existing procedural generator for a bounded set of hostile human quest camps while keeping the quest itself intact. The player must be able to arrive, fight, rescue or complete the objective, receive the original reward, leave, and observe the original success/failure/expiry behavior. A prettier camp that breaks its quest is a failed adapter.

Implement two initial contracts in sequence, after inspecting their resolved definitions and reproducing native behavior:

| Contract | Initial target | Required distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Camp elimination | Core `BanditCamp` site part and its native quest | Preserve the authoritative hostile force and native defeat/reward signals; a synthetic map without a real quest is insufficient validation. |
| Guarded prisoner rescue | Core `PrisonerWillingToJoin` plus `Outpost` | Place the existing protected prisoner in a reachable, enclosed prison area, separately from the hostile garrison; preserve identity, custody, rescue/join behavior and quest ownership. |

Support means an explicitly verified **whole site-part combination**, faction/generator contract and arrival path, not any site containing one familiar part. In P4D.0 record the actual quest script, ordered parts, workers, gen steps, parameter sources and lifecycle callbacks for each supported combination. A plain `Outpost` may reuse the implementation, but enable it only for a documented verified contract. Guarded `ItemStash` is a follow-on candidate to document, not a third required adapter. Unknown markers, ambushes, extra turret/threat parts, downed-refugee sites, natural-resource sites, ruins, nonhuman forces, DLC-specific objectives/maps and third-party generators stay native unless separately included and validated. Do not reveal hidden threat details in player-facing diagnostics.

Use a small strategy pattern for objective-specific eligibility/reservations/placement, with shared planning and transaction services. See [P4D findings](p4d-findings.md).

Deliver at least the camp-elimination adapter with real end-to-end evidence. Prisoner rescue is part of the authorized work; if it encounters a genuine identity/lifecycle blocker, retain native behavior and record the exact blocker, leaving that checkpoint open rather than marking all P4D complete. Do not broaden the support list merely to claim coverage. No blanket `Site` replacement or new general quest framework.

## Source baseline and integration hazards

Inspection of installed Core 1.6 definitions and decompiled 1.6.4871 code establishes the following starting points, which implementation must verify against resolved runtime behavior:

- `BanditCamp` inherits the `Outpost` part definition. Both link to `GenStep_Outpost` at order 400 and power at 401. That step reads `sitePart.parms.threatPoints`, `expectedEnemyCount`, the outpost pawn-group seed and `lootMarketValue`; it also uses `UsedRects`/`RectOfInterest` and flags controlling pawn generation, farms and other features.
- `PrisonerWillingToJoin` links its own step at order 1600. The step takes the existing pawn from the site-part holder when present, builds a prison cell, spawns that pawn and sets rescue/join behavior and fog/interest state. Simply running it after our camp can create a second prison elsewhere, and suppressing it without replacing its obligations can lose the prisoner.
- `Site` owns callbacks and native threat/completion signals, including `AllEnemiesDefeated` and `NoActiveThreats`, plus map/world-object removal rules. The bandit quest uses the native defeat signal. Keep those owners authoritative.
- Our current selection hook targets exact ordinary `Settlement` objects, and planning/state initialization contains `Settlement`-specific assumptions. Keep the ordinary guard intact and add a narrow quest path rather than widening it to arbitrary map parents. P4C terrain/rock finishes are part of the current production transaction.

Relevant local source names: `RimWorld.GenStep_Outpost.cs`, `RimWorld.GenStep_PrisonerWillingToJoin.cs`, `RimWorld.Planet.Site.cs`, `RimWorld.SitePartWorker_Outpost.cs`, and `RimWorld.Planet.SitePartWorker_PrisonerWillingToJoin.cs` under the pinned decompile directory. Inspect the corresponding `SitePart` workers, quest XML, pawn/Lord generation and arrival/removal callbacks as needed. Source names are an investigation map, not a mandate to patch each class.

## Quest contract and planning

Build a small immutable input adapter around existing planner requirements. Record the site/part identity, supported contract version, faction/stage, saved deterministic seed, authoritative threat and loot parameters, required pawn/object references or stable IDs, required objective areas, arrival constraints and supported generator steps. Runtime references stay outside the pure planner; give pure planning only the facts and reservations it needs.

**Roster and threat:** use the quest's own pawn-generation budget, seed, group kind and scripted occupants. Establish how `expectedEnemyCount` relates to actual generation; do not assume it is always an exact count. Generate or obtain the authoritative roster once, size facilities for that roster, and preserve required pawn kinds/equipment/identity constraints. Do not resample until an easier roster fits, apply ordinary wealth rolls or the four-to-twelve/mean-seven cap, create unfunded workers/mortar crew, or silently trim quest defenders. If a legitimate roster exceeds supported planner capacity, retain native generation with a reason. Add optional defenses only within a declared quest-derived allowance, avoiding double counting native turret/threat parts or a hidden ordinary-settlement budget. Report resulting pawn and static-defense commitments; full P6 difficulty tuning remains separate.

**Facilities:** reuse procedural room, terrain, defense and finish planning with a compact camp profile. Include appropriate beds/barracks, food/storage/support and objective space for the actual occupants. Reserve prisoner cells/doors and required items or buildings before packing. An explicitly short-lived quest outpost need not grow a full agricultural compound; support a deliberate finite-supply/no-field profile where the native contract permits it, without treating failed ordinary field placement as proof of that profile. Field-dependent P4B assumptions must either gain a narrow validated quest path or cause native fallback; do not fake an empty agricultural enclosure.

**Protected content:** reuse the planner's existing prison room and `PrisonBed` fixtures for the quest target, with adequate reserved capacity and validated custody, enclosure and access. Do not add a separate prison generator or let the native step create a duplicate cell elsewhere. The quest prisoner is not a newly generated substitute. Preserve their thing ID, name, health, relations, guest/custody state, quest tags, rescue flags and holder ownership as applicable to native behavior. Required items/buildings likewise retain identity, quality, stuff, stack counts and signal-relevant components. Separate these objects from disposable mod-owned decoration and from the controlled hostile roster. Prisoners, rescue targets, visitors, animals and player pawns must never enter P3 merely because they share a faction or map. Scripted waves/escape behavior keep their native owner unless that exact behavior is adapted and tested.

**Rewards and loot:** preserve the quest's existing reward objects and selection, native loot allowance and reward timing. Do not turn settlement trade stocks or ordinary wealth into bonus quest rewards. Distinguish reward objects, site loot and finite operating supplies in the implementation ledger; no duplicated holder transfer or repeated reward on reload/re-entry. Canonical settlement trade remains P5.

## Generation, failure and lifecycle contract

1. Select an adapter only after the entire site composition, faction and generator/required-step contract is recognized. Respect the existing mod enablement and other generator ownership. Unsupported sites run their original pipeline unchanged with a concise developer diagnostic. Never adapt already generated maps retroactively.
2. Identify the narrow replacement point and validate actual ordering. Replace only verified layout/power/objective-placement work; retain terrain generation, other required steps and native `SitePart` lifecycle callbacks. Track `UsedRects`, `RectOfInterest`, fog/unfog roots and arrival constraints intentionally. Do not run duplicate outpost/prison/defender steps or invoke a full generator recursively as fallback.
3. Preflight objective reservations, facility capacity, roof/terrain preservation, reachable entry, prison access and final defense/service paths before consuming protected holders or suppressing a native step. For supported normal caravan entry, the player must be able to reach the objective through normal door/breach gameplay. Do not open enemy doors or reveal all fog to pass access checks. Keep unsupported transport/space/gravship contracts native until proven.
4. Extend the shared transaction with explicit borrowed quest-object ownership. On partial failure, return each borrowed pawn/item to its original holder with its original state, remove only created content, restore terrain/rock/roof/plant/finish mutations and generation metadata, and detach any newly created controller. Do not destroy and recreate a named target. Prevent transient damage/despawn/ownership operations from sending irreversible quest outcomes; choose a safe commit boundary before publishing those effects.
5. Native fallback is allowed only after verified rollback to a state in which the original steps can run once with their required context and random-state semantics. Use purpose-separated/random-state scopes for planning. Test zero-mutation rejection and late failure after protected-content placement. If safe recovery cannot be guaranteed for a contract, leave that adapter disabled; never swallow the failure and claim a playable quest.
6. Keep native success, failure, timeout, reward, reinforcement and map/world-object cleanup mechanisms authoritative. P3 controls only the admitted generated combat roster and relinquishes control on death, capture, faction change or departure. Native threat checks must still account for living/downed/fleeing enemies and hostile turrets according to the actual game rules; do not manually emit success merely because a local garrison count reaches zero.
7. Store only the site-scoped adapter/seed/commit state needed for stable generation and active save/reload. Distinguish quest-site state from ordinary settlement wealth/state and clean up references with the native site lifecycle. Re-entry must not duplicate targets, defenders, loot or rewards. Test the supported site's actual leave/remove/revisit behavior, including the case where native removal makes re-entry impossible; do not impose ordinary-settlement regeneration or keep a quest map alive to bypass its rules. This necessary quest correctness is in P4D even though general settlement revisit persistence remains P5.

## Validation and exit

- Pure fixtures cover exact supported part combinations and an added unsupported part, deterministic replay, low/high threat inputs, roster capacity rejection, missing target/holder, conflicting objective reservations, and an unbuildable site. Assert that ordinary settlement budget/population selection is unchanged.
- Use actual native quests generated in the disposable game profile, not only a debug-spawned `Site`. Record quest/site/part IDs, authoritative parameters, target IDs, seeds, supported composition, controller membership, native signals, reward delivery and map-removal outcome. Test actions exercise native completion; logs cannot substitute for observing the quest state.
- For elimination: accept, arrive, fight, satisfy native defeat conditions, receive the original reward exactly once, and depart. Also test expiry/failure where the native contract defines it, leaving before completion, active save/reload and turret/remaining-enemy effects on success detection.
- For rescue: verify the original target in its planned prison, rescue/join behavior, target death/failure as defined by the quest, departure with and without the prisoner, and save/reload before and after release. Check custody/identity/quest tags and no P3 control of the target. An outcome that native Core treats as neutral must remain neutral rather than inventing a new failure signal.
- Force rejection before mutation, failure after base creation, and failure after a protected-object transfer. Compare holder contents, identities, quest state, generation bookkeeping and terrain/rock/roof/plant/finish state; prove native fallback or retry once without duplicate occupants or outcomes.
- Include tribal/industrial where supported, open ground and constrained terrain, and a representative available Geological Landforms case. Missing optional-mod evidence is reported precisely. Unsupported parts, nonhuman/peaceful/DLC/competing generators and ordinary settlements retain their original routing. No traps/IEDs are added by supported procedural generation; retaining an unsupported site's native pipeline is not authorization to alter that site's original content.
- Run relevant complete planner/layout suites, new adapter tests and Release build; follow existing disposable-game skills and verified process ownership. No normal profiles/saves, new tooling installation, broad quest simulator or additional agents are required.

**Exit:** both listed initial quest contracts have explicit support entries and real normal/rejection/recovery/reload/lifecycle evidence, with their original targets, threat inputs, rewards and signals preserved. Unsupported combinations remain native and diagnosable. The production shared planner supplies the layout, finite defenses and P4C finishes without duplicating quest content or changing ordinary settlement behavior. Do not mark P4D complete from compilation, a generic site screenshot or elimination-only evidence.
